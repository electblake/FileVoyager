**The strongest fit is Qt’s built-in model/view stack: `QFileSystemModel` + `QTreeView` + `QListView`.** These already provide filesystem browsing and file-manager-style icon layouts. The remaining work is connecting your thumbnails and wiring the View/Sort actions. [Qt 6.12.0](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qfilesystemmodel.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Explorer’s View and Sort menus still need application wiring:**

- **Large/medium/small icons:** change the list view’s `iconSize` and `gridSize`.
- **List/details:** switch between the configured list and details views.
- **Sort by / ascending / descending:** call the filesystem or proxy model’s `sort(column, order)`.

Those operations exist already; Qt does not assemble them into a complete Explorer menu for you. [Qt for Python](https://doc.qt.io/qtforpython-6/PySide6/QtWidgets/QListView.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Connecting your existing Windows thumbnails

Supply the retrieved thumbnail through the model’s **`Qt.ItemDataRole.DecorationRole`** as a `QImage`, `QPixmap` or `QIcon`. Qt’s default item delegate already renders those types in its views. Your Windows thumbnail-cache loader supplies the images; the Qt models and views handle browsing, selection, layout and sorting. [Qt 6.12.0](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qstyleditemdelegate.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
